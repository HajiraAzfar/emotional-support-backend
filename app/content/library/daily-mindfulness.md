Mindfulness has collected a lot of mystique it does not need. It means paying attention to what is happening now, on purpose, without deciding whether it is good or bad. That is the whole of it.

## Three that take a minute

- **The first sip.** Whatever you drink first in the morning — notice the temperature, the smell, the weight of the cup. One minute.
- **Feet on the floor.** Sitting anywhere: notice where your feet touch the ground, where your back touches the chair. Nothing has to change.
- **One breath, counted.** In for four, out for six. Six times.

## When the mind wanders

It will, within seconds, and that is not failure — noticing that it wandered *is* the practice. Bring it back. It will go again. Bring it back again.

## What it is not for

It is not a way to stop feeling something. If you sit quietly and grief arrives, mindfulness has worked exactly as intended. It is not a substitute for treatment, and if quiet attention makes distress worse — which happens for some people, especially after trauma — stop, open your eyes, and move instead.
