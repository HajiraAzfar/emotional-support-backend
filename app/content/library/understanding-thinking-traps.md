A thinking trap is a shortcut the mind takes when it is tired, frightened or hurt. It is not a flaw in you, and it is not a sign that something is wrong with your mind. Everyone's mind takes these shortcuts. They only become a problem when the same one runs so often that it starts to feel like the truth rather than a thought.

## Why noticing helps

Nothing has to be argued with. The first step is simply seeing the pattern as a pattern: "that is the one where I decide what someone else is thinking", rather than "she is annoyed with me". The thought loses a little of its grip as soon as it has a name, because a named thought is something you have, not something you are.

## What noticing is not

It is not telling yourself the thought is wrong. Sometimes a worry is accurate, and being told to think positively about a real problem is not help, it is dismissal. The question is never "is this thought good or bad" but "is this the whole picture".

## Trying it

When a heavy thought arrives, write it down in the words it came in — not a tidied version. Then ask one question: what else could also be true? You are not looking for a cheerful answer. You are looking for whether anything is missing.

Some days nothing else will seem true. That is worth writing down too.
